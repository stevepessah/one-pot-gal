No one knows how this ancient meal came to be known as “Wok Chicken.” Perhaps it was because it was made in a 1970s electric wok. Perhaps it was because it contained chicken.

Whatever the reason, it was the quickest way for our family to embrace multiculturalism.

![Vernon Family Wok Chicken](images/vernon-family-wok-chicken-1.jpg)

## Ingredients

- 1 tablespoon oil (the best is peanut oil...but you know we never had that in our cabinets...we used some classy Canola)
- 2 to 3 cloves garlic, minced
- 1 tablespoon minced ginger
- 1 pound boneless, skinless chicken breasts, cut into strips
- 1 medium onion, diced
- 2 cups sliced carrots
- 1 red bell pepper, seeded and sliced into thin strips
- 2 cups sugar snap peas
- 1 (15 ounce) can baby corn, drained
- 2 cups broccoli florets
- ¼ cup soy sauce
- 2 teaspoons cornstarch
- 1 cup chicken broth
- Rice, rice baby!

## Instructions

- Heat oil in a wok (preferably electric, for nostalgia’s sake) over medium-high heat. Add garlic and ginger, and cook for 1 minute. Add chicken and cook for 3 to 4 minutes, until starting to brown. Stir!
- Add onions, carrots, and peppers and cook 1 minute. Add snap peas, corn and broccoli and cook 2 minutes.
- Hope that you have been timing this all perfectly.
- Add soy sauce and cook 2 minutes, until vegetables are crisp.
- Dissolve cornstarch in chicken broth in a small bowl and add to wok. Simmer for two minutes until the sauce thickens.
- Serve over rice.
